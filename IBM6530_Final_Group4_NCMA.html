Deliverable 2 — Final Quarto Report

Narrative Summary of the Business Problem

NCMA SGV is a nonprofit, membership-based professional association chapter serving contract management professionals, students, and early-career talent across the San Gabriel Valley. The chapter delivers value through professional development events, educational programming, networking, certification support, and leadership engagement.

The core business question: Are NCMA SGV's website, social events, and digital channels effectively reaching the right audiences — particularly Gen Z and early-career professionals in the San Gabriel Valley — while also retaining established members?

To answer this, NCMA SGV needs visibility into the full user journey — acquisition, engagement, conversion, and retention. Metrics such as traffic source, geography, landing-page engagement, event-registration rate, subscription-conversion rate, returning users, and retention rate reveal which channels and experiences drive meaningful participation. Ultimately the problem is converting digital interest into measurable organizational growth.

Descriptive Insights (BigQuery / mart_daily_acquisition_v1)

1. Traffic Over Time

Insight: Sessions are highly volatile rather than steadily growing. A major peak in late March pushed daily sessions above 150, followed by a sharp drop to under 50 in early April and another spike mid-April. This pattern suggests event-driven traffic bursts rather than sustainable baseline acquisition.

4. Traffic by Device

Insight: Desktop drives the majority of session volume, consistent with a professional/B2B audience. However, on the engagement-rate side, mobile (~42%) materially outperforms desktop (~5%), suggesting that mobile users who do arrive interact far more meaningfully.

5. Engagement Rate by Channel

Insight: "Other" leads engagement, followed by Referral and Organic Search (~55–58%). Direct sits at the bottom (~5–10%) despite being the largest channel — reinforcing that the highest-volume traffic is also the lowest-quality.
Key Takeaway: The site relies heavily on Direct traffic that is largely low-intent and geographically off-target (Singapore-dominant, near-zero engagement). Smaller channels — Organic Search, Referral, "Other" — convert engagement at 5–10× the rate of Direct. Traffic patterns are spike-driven (event windows) rather than steady. NCMA SGV's real high-quality audience is a thin slice: U.S. users on mobile and desktop arriving via Referral and Organic Search.

Advanced Analysis (Attribution)

Comparing last-click attribution against multi-touch (data-driven / position-based) modeling reveals that Direct's apparent dominance overstates its true contribution. Upper-funnel channels — Organic Search and Referral — receive disproportionately little credit under last-click despite higher engagement rates and likely assist behavior. A multi-touch view re-weights credit toward these supporting channels and reframes media-allocation decisions.

Recommendations

  1. Reallocate budget toward high-efficiency channels, not high-volume ones. Direct delivers volume but minimal engagement; Referral and Organic Search are under-funded relative to their conversion quality. Shift incremental spend toward channels with positive marginal return.
  2. Adopt multi-touch attribution as the standard reporting framework. Move away from last-click to a data-driven or position-based model so upper-funnel interactions are not undervalued.
  3. Filter and investigate Singapore traffic. 2,989 sessions at 0% engagement is the largest distortion in every KPI. Treat as suspected bot/non-target, segment it out of dashboards, and verify with Google's invalid-traffic signals.
  4. Optimize underperformers via A/B testing, not elimination. Test messaging, targeting, and landing-page alignment before cutting channels — weak performance may be an execution issue, not a channel-viability issue.
  5. Strengthen tracking consistency. Standardize event names, ensure complete UTM/parameter capture, and align GA4 ↔ BigQuery ↔ reporting layers.
  6. Establish a recurring dashboard review cadence tied to campaign cycles and chapter goals so reporting drives decisions rather than sitting static.

Limitations

  • Aggregation level: Mart-level data limits visibility into individual user journeys.
  • Attribution uncertainty: No model fully captures real-world decision-making; outputs are estimates.
  • Source discrepancies: GA4 UI vs. BigQuery exports differ due to latency, sampling, and filtering.
  • Tracking gaps: Missing UTMs, inconsistent tagging, consent-mode restrictions, cross-domain breaks.
  • Short window: ~60 days (Mar 2 – May 1, 2026); seasonality effects not captured.
  • Offline/external influences: Brand awareness, word of mouth, and offline events fall outside the digital frame.
